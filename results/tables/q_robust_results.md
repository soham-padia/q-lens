## Setup, checks and related work

**Setup.** Push `s*alpha*rho_l*u` (unit u, s = +-1, alpha in {0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.4}) at every valid position (16..T-2) of source block l (D1 convention). Predictor: `ratio = sqrt(sum_c |Q_c|^2 / sum_c |J_c u|^2)` with J, Q the exact first and second derivatives (nested jvp) of the centred logits at every valid position, on 8 fit contexts (fineweb docs 1500+). Q is the full second derivative, so along u the second-order logit term equals the first-order one at alpha = 2/ratio. All outcomes are behavioural and on 32 held-out contexts: next-token KL(clean||pushed) and top-1 flips at every valid position; ~100 few-shot capital facts (push at 16..T-1, accuracy at the last token); greedy 20-token continuations on 16 contexts for a direction subset (token edit distance from the clean continuation, NLL under the unpushed model). Classes: iso (random), data (centred residuals), svd (top right-singular vectors of J), sharp (top Hessian eigenvectors of the next-token loss w.r.t. the push), maxr (gradient ascent of the ratio), ctrl (same fit |J u|, low ratio, one per top-ratio direction). Fragility = dose alpha* at which a sign-averaged measure crosses a threshold (primary: KL 0.1, flip 0.2, facts error 0.1, edit 0.5; censored at > 0.4, ranked last). `-log dose` is larger for more fragile directions.

**Score-hacking guard.** A Q-derived direction once moved a readout without changing the model's state. Here the ratio is only a predictor; every claim below is checked against KL, flips, fact accuracy or continuations.

**Related work.** Azizi et al. 2026 (arXiv 2610.16084, NeurIPS 2026) compute a steering Hessian of the next-token loss with respect to a steering vector at one layer (Lanczos on finite-difference HVPs, 7-8B models) and find that sharp directions anti-steer. Their object is the curvature of one scalar (the loss) along the push; ours is the exact second-order term Q of the whole logit map (every position, every token), normalised by the first-order term, so |Q|/|J| measures nonlinearity rather than loss sharpness: a direction can be sharp for the loss because J u is large (Gauss-Newton part), with a small ratio. The `sharp` class here reproduces their directions with exact HVPs (forward-over-reverse) so the two can be compared on the same behavioural tests.
